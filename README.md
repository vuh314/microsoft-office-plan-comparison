## One-time purchase versus subscription: the differences that actually cost you something

A Microsoft 365 subscription is a recurring payment that keeps your apps current. Office 2024 is a single up-front payment for apps on one computer, and it will not receive new features. Microsoft states plainly that one-time purchases have no upgrade option: when the next major release arrives, you buy it again at full price. Security updates do continue, so the software is not abandoned, it is just frozen at the 2024 feature set.

Cost structure is the visible difference. The ones that surprise people are quieter:

- **Devices.** A one-time licence installs once, on either a PC or a Mac. A subscription installs on all your devices and lets you sign in on five at the same time, across PC, Mac, tablet and phone.
- **Sharing.** Family, Premium and Pro can be shared with up to five other people; that is six people total including you. Personal is one person only. A one-time licence cannot be shared at all.
- **Storage.** Office 2024 includes no extra online storage. Personal gives 1TB; Family and Premium give 2TB; Pro gives 5TB. Every subscription tier can be expanded up to 10TB.
- **Mobile editing.** With a one-time purchase you can install the mobile apps free, but on tablets and phones under 10.1 inches you get basic editing only. Signing in with a subscription unlocks the extra features.
- **Support.** One-time buyers get support resources and chat, with call-back limited to activation. Subscribers get ongoing call-back and chat support, and that support also covers Windows 10 and Windows 11.

If you keep a PC for five or six years, use it for one job, and never touch Copilot, the buyout is cheaper over that span by definition, because the payments stop. If you want the next version when it lands, a subscription is the only path that includes it.

## Which plan matches your situation

One person on a single laptop who wants current apps, Copilot and 1TB of OneDrive: **Personal**. It is the smallest subscription that includes the desktop apps.

Two adults and three kids, all needing Word and Excel on school laptops: **Family**. Six accounts under one payment beats buying three or four one-time licences, and the shared storage pool means you are not rationing 1TB between five people.

You write, research or build decks with Copilot daily: **Premium**. Microsoft describes it as including extensive AI usage and access to exclusive Copilot features, which matters only if you hit limits on the standard plans. If you open Copilot a few times a week, that extra headroom is money you will not recover.

Six people who store video projects or large photo libraries: **Pro**, with its 5TB pool. For a normal household, 2TB on Family is the sensible ceiling.

One desktop, classic apps, email in Outlook, no interest in AI or cloud storage: **Office Home & Business 2024**. It includes Word, Excel, PowerPoint, Outlook and OneNote for Windows 11, Windows 10 or macOS, and it works with Teams. Confirm the app list on the Office Home 2024 product page if you are considering the cheaper package, since its inclusions differ from the Business edition.

Storage and an ad-free inbox, no desktop software: **Basic**. Note that the official comparison lists 100GB of storage, ad-free email, secure photo and file storage and support, and does **not** list desktop applications. Do not buy Basic expecting to install Word.

## The limits that are easy to miss

**AI is not shared.** Family, Premium and Pro include Copilot, but the AI features are available to the subscription owner only. Adding five family members gives them the apps and the storage; it does not give them Copilot. Basic has no AI features at all.

**Copilot in Excel needs AutoSave.** It works only when AutoSave is on, which means the file must be saved to OneDrive. Unsaved local files get no Copilot help in Excel.

**Subscriptions need a monthly check-in.** Install and activate, and you can work offline, but connect at least once a month or the apps drop into reduced-functionality mode: you can view and print, not edit or create. Reconnect and sign in to restore them.

**Office 2024 needs reactivation.** The one-time version installs and activates over the internet, then works offline, but it reactivates periodically, and hardware changes can trigger it. Miss the reactivation and you may see reduced functionality or an unlicensed product error. If the machine genuinely cannot reach the internet, Microsoft points people to Office LTSC instead.

**Deactivating a device makes it read-only.** At the five-device limit, you can deactivate a device in your Microsoft account to free a slot. Office on that device switches to read-only: documents open, editing stops. Nothing you saved locally is lost.

**32-bit may be the right install.** Microsoft recommends the 32-bit version even on 64-bit Windows, because add-ins and other applications tend to be more compatible with it. You cannot run 32-bit and 64-bit Office on the same computer.

**Mac users lose Access.** Word, Excel, PowerPoint, Outlook and OneNote are available on macOS; Access is not. Publisher is a PC-only inclusion as well.

**Small offices can stack licences.** Microsoft's support documentation notes that a user can activate up to 25 licences of Office Home 2024, Office Home & Business 2024 and Office Professional 2021, which covers a small team without a business plan.

## Pooled storage changes what a family plan is worth

Microsoft is moving shared subscriptions from per-person allowances to one shared pool. From **8 October 2026**, all new Microsoft 365 Family and Premium subscribers, including people repurchasing after expiry, get 2TB of pooled OneDrive storage; Pro subscribers get 5TB pooled. From **2 May 2027**, existing Family and Premium subscribers receive 2TB pooled at renewal.

Pooled means the allowance is shared across everyone on the subscription. Privacy does not change: each person's mailbox, files and folders stay private to them. So a family of six on Family is not six people each holding 2TB, it is 2TB divided however the household actually uses it, expandable to 10TB if you pay for more.

## How to purchase Microsoft Office and get it activated

1. Buy from the Microsoft Store or an authorised retailer. Online orders usually deliver the key by email or print it on the receipt; boxed copies have it inside the packaging.
2. Go to **setup.office.com** and sign in with a Microsoft account, or create one. The account is what holds the licence, so use one you will keep.
3. Enter the 25-character key, formatted like XXXXX-XXXXX-XXXXX-XXXXX-XXXXX. You redeem it once.
4. Install from your Microsoft account page. Keep the key somewhere safe as proof of purchase if you ever need support.
5. Stay online through install and activation. After that, Office 2024 runs offline with periodic reactivation; subscriptions run offline with a monthly connection.

Buying in the wrong country is a real failure mode. Microsoft warns that selecting a billing country different from where you will use the software can break subscription setup and renewal.

## The $29.99 "lifetime licence" question

Third-party price aggregators list Office packages from $7.99 up to $159.99, including several sellers offering "Microsoft Office 2024 Pro Plus, 3 PC lifetime licence" at $29.99. Those product names do not match Microsoft's consumer line-up, which is Office Home 2024 and Office Home & Business 2024, and "lifetime licence" is not a term Microsoft uses for the one-time purchase, which carries no upgrade rights. Prices for the same Office Home 2024 package also swing widely between resellers.

Cheap keys are not automatically fraudulent, but you are buying from a reseller, not from Microsoft, and the licensing terms may not survive an audit or a hardware change. If the price looks like a fraction of the official one, check who the seller is and what the licence actually covers before paying.

## When you do not need to buy anything yet

Microsoft 365 Online is free in a browser with a Microsoft account, and it covers most everyday editing in Word, Excel and PowerPoint. Advanced features sit behind the desktop apps. If your work is light documents and spreadsheets, start there and spend nothing. A free trial of Microsoft 365 also exists; the length and any payment requirements were not specified on the page I checked, so read the terms at sign-up.

Buy when you need desktop apps, offline editing, Copilot, more than 100GB of storage, or the mobile extras. Not before.
